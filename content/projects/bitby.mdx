## Context

bitby is a **UI reconstruction study**: a student learning project that rebuilds
the interface patterns of the Binance mobile app.
It is **not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to Binance**, reproduces none of their trademarks or logos, and is not a working exchange, with no real market data, no accounts, and no trades.

The reason to rebuild a trading interface is that it is genuinely difficult.
Dense numeric rows, constant updates, tight vertical rhythm, and a hierarchy
that has to survive being read at a glance are harder problems than most CRUD
screens ever pose.

## My role

This was my first large project, and I was new to almost everything involved
in it. It was a team effort: three frontend developers, three backend developers
and a project manager.

I worked on the frontend, including the screens, the navigation, and the list
and chart components. The app is built in React Native, and the backend
developers built the Java backend. The `stack` above lists only what I worked
on, which is the same rule every project on this site follows.

## Key decisions

**Rebuilding from observation, not from assets.** Working out the spacing, type
scale and hierarchy by eye is what made this a study rather than a copy-paste
exercise, and it is where the learning actually was.

## The hard part

The hardest problem came when we connected the frontend to the backend. Each
side worked perfectly on its own, but once we joined them they would not talk
to each other, and for a while we honestly did not know why.

The cause was on the frontend. It contained logic that stopped the calls to the
backend from completing successfully, so the backend never received a request
it could answer. Removing that logic fixed it. What I took from it is to connect
the two halves early, rather than assuming that two pieces which each work alone
will work together.

## Outcome, and what I would do differently

The app was not bundled for distribution, so there is nothing to install. The
source is on GitHub.

What I took from it was calibration. Matching a production interface by eye
teaches how much of a polished product is spacing and type discipline rather
than features.

I do not regret how it turned out, but if I built it again I would approach the
design differently from the start, bringing in the design patterns and
architectural knowledge I have now to keep the frontend logic cleaner.
